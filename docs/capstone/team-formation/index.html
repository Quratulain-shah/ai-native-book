Capstone Team Formation Guidelines

Team Formation Overview​

The capstone project requires students to work in teams of 2-3 members to design, implement, and demonstrate an autonomous humanoid robot system. Effective team formation is critical to project success, as it determines the distribution of responsibilities, communication patterns, and overall project execution quality.

Team Size and Composition​

Team Size​

  • Minimum: 2 students per team
  • Maximum: 3 students per team
  • Rationale: Small teams enable close collaboration while providing sufficient resources for complex projects

Role Distribution​

Teams should consider specializing roles based on individual strengths and interests:

Perception Specialist

  • Focus: Computer vision, sensor fusion, object detection
  • Skills: OpenCV, neural networks, sensor calibration
  • Responsibilities: Developing and optimizing perception systems

Navigation Specialist

  • Focus: Path planning, locomotion, motion control
  • Skills: ROS 2 navigation, control theory, kinematics
  • Responsibilities: Implementing navigation and movement systems

AI/Interaction Specialist

  • Focus: Natural language processing, human-robot interaction
  • Skills: NLP, machine learning, user interface design
  • Responsibilities: Developing AI interaction and decision-making systems
note

Teams may distribute roles differently based on member strengths, but all teams must ensure comprehensive coverage of perception, navigation, and AI interaction capabilities.

Team Formation Process​

Step 1: Skills Assessment (Day 1)​

Before forming teams, students should complete a self-assessment of their technical skills:

Technical Skills Matrix​

Hardware Experience

Interest Areas​

  • Perception & Sensing: Computer vision, sensor fusion, SLAM
  • Navigation & Control: Path planning, locomotion, motion control
  • AI & Interaction: NLP, decision making, human-robot interaction
  • Systems & Integration: Architecture, testing, safety protocols

Step 2: Team Matching (Day 2)​

Self-Selected Teams​

  • Students may form teams based on pre-existing relationships or shared interests
  • Teams must ensure complementary skill sets across all required areas
  • Maximum team size is strictly enforced

Instructor-Assisted Matching​

  • For students who prefer guidance, instructors will facilitate team formation
  • Matching considers skill balance, interest alignment, and scheduling compatibility
  • Students may request specific role preferences during matching

Step 3: Team Contract Development (Day 3)​

All teams must develop and sign a team contract outlining expectations and responsibilities:

Contract Elements​

  • Communication Protocols: Meeting schedules, communication channels, response time expectations
  • Work Distribution: Role assignments, task allocation, workload balance
  • Conflict Resolution: Process for addressing disagreements or performance issues
  • Commitment Level: Expected time investment and attendance requirements
  • Quality Standards: Definition of acceptable work quality and peer review processes

Team Dynamics and Communication​

Communication Framework​

Daily Standups​

  • Frequency: Daily during implementation phases
  • Duration: 15 minutes maximum
  • Format: Each member reports progress, blockers, and next steps
  • Platform: In-person or video conference

Weekly Reviews​

  • Frequency: Weekly formal team meetings
  • Duration: 1 hour
  • Agenda: Progress review, milestone assessment, planning for next week
  • Documentation: Meeting notes shared with instructor

Communication Channels​

  • Primary: Team messaging platform (Slack, Discord, or Teams)
  • Secondary: Email for formal communications
  • Emergency: Direct contact information for urgent issues

Decision-Making Process​

Consensus-Based Decisions​

  • Technical architecture decisions
  • System design choices
  • Resource allocation

Designated Leader Decisions​

  • Daily task assignments
  • Meeting scheduling
  • External communications

Escalation Process​

  • Team disagreement → Team discussion and compromise
  • Unresolved disagreement → Instructor consultation
  • Major conflicts → Academic advisor involvement

Role Expectations and Responsibilities​

Shared Responsibilities (All Team Members)​

  • Attendance: Attend all team meetings and project sessions
  • Communication: Respond to team communications within 24 hours
  • Quality: Maintain high standards for all deliverables
  • Safety: Adhere to all safety protocols and procedures
  • Documentation: Maintain clear, comprehensive project documentation
  • Testing: Participate in comprehensive system testing

Role-Specific Expectations​

Perception Specialist​

  • Implement and optimize computer vision algorithms
  • Calibrate and validate sensor systems
  • Develop sensor fusion capabilities
  • Ensure real-time performance of perception systems
  • Integrate perception outputs with navigation and AI systems

Navigation Specialist​

  • Implement path planning and locomotion algorithms
  • Develop motion control systems
  • Integrate with robot hardware platforms
  • Ensure safe and efficient navigation
  • Optimize for real-world performance

AI/Interaction Specialist​

  • Develop natural language processing capabilities
  • Implement human-robot interaction systems
  • Design decision-making algorithms
  • Ensure ethical and safe AI behavior
  • Integrate with perception and navigation systems

Project Management Structure​

Milestone-Based Planning​

Teams should break the project into milestone-based phases with clear deliverables:

Phase 1: System Design (Week 11, Days 1-2)​

  • Complete system architecture design
  • Define component interfaces and data flow
  • Establish testing protocols
  • Create project timeline and task assignments

Phase 2: Component Development (Week 11, Days 3-5)​

  • Develop individual system components
  • Conduct unit testing for each component
  • Integrate components in simulation environment
  • Validate safety protocols

Phase 3: System Integration (Week 12)​

  • Integrate all components into complete system
  • Conduct comprehensive testing in simulation
  • Optimize system performance
  • Prepare for physical deployment

Phase 4: Physical Deployment (Week 13)​

  • Deploy system on physical hardware
  • Conduct real-world testing and validation
  • Optimize for physical platform constraints
  • Prepare for final demonstration

Task Management Tools​

  • Issue Tracking: Use GitHub Issues or similar for task management
  • Progress Tracking: Regular updates on completed tasks and blockers
  • Version Control: Proper Git workflow with clear commit messages
  • Documentation: Maintain living documentation throughout project

Conflict Resolution and Performance Management​

Addressing Performance Issues​

Early Warning Signs​

  • Missed deadlines or deliverables
  • Poor quality work consistently
  • Lack of communication or participation
  • Safety protocol violations

Intervention Process​

  1. Peer Feedback: Team members provide direct feedback to struggling member
  2. Team Meeting: Formal discussion of performance issues and solutions
  3. Instructor Consultation: Seek guidance from instructor or TA
  4. Role Adjustment: Modify responsibilities if appropriate
  5. Academic Intervention: Escalate to academic advisor if necessary

Conflict Resolution Framework​

Minor Disagreements​

  • Direct discussion between involved parties
  • Focus on technical merits rather than personal preferences
  • Seek compromise solutions
  • Document decisions for future reference

Major Conflicts​

  • Involvement of team mediator (instructor or TA)
  • Structured discussion with clear agenda
  • Focus on project success rather than individual preferences
  • Written agreement on resolution path

Safety and Ethical Considerations​

Team Safety Protocols​

  • Shared Responsibility: All team members responsible for safety compliance
  • Training Completion: All members must complete safety training
  • Equipment Handling: Proper procedures for hardware access and operation
  • Emergency Procedures: Clear understanding of emergency stop and evacuation procedures

Ethical Collaboration​

  • Academic Integrity: All work must represent team's own efforts
  • Attribution: Properly cite all external resources and code
  • Inclusive Environment: Respectful treatment of all team members
  • Professional Conduct: Maintain professional standards in all interactions

Assessment and Evaluation​

Team-Based Evaluation​

  • Technical Implementation: 70% of grade based on collective technical achievement
  • Collaboration: 20% of grade based on team dynamics and collaboration
  • Individual Contribution: 10% of grade based on individual effort and growth

Individual Accountability​

  • Peer Evaluation: Team members evaluate each other's contributions
  • Individual Demonstrations: Each member demonstrates understanding of their components
  • Personal Reflection: Individual reflection on learning and contribution
  • Professional Growth: Assessment of individual skill development

Resources and Support​

Team Formation Resources​

  • Team Building Workshop: Optional workshop on effective team collaboration
  • Communication Training: Resources on effective technical communication
  • Conflict Resolution: Training on addressing team conflicts constructively
  • Project Management: Tools and templates for effective project management

Ongoing Support​

  • Team Mentor: Graduate student mentor assigned to each team
  • Faculty Office Hours: Regular availability for team consultation
  • Technical Support: Dedicated support for hardware and software issues
  • Safety Officer: Available for safety protocol questions and concerns

Success Factors​

Characteristics of Successful Teams​

  • Complementary Skills: Team members with diverse but complementary technical skills
  • Clear Communication: Regular, structured communication patterns
  • Shared Vision: Common understanding of project goals and success criteria
  • Mutual Respect: Respect for each other's contributions and expertise
  • Flexibility: Willingness to adapt roles and approaches as needed
  • Commitment: Strong commitment to project success and team collaboration

Common Challenges and Solutions​

  • Skill Imbalance: Address through mentorship and skill development
  • Communication Issues: Implement structured communication protocols
  • Workload Distribution: Regular assessment and rebalancing of responsibilities
  • Technical Conflicts: Focus on technical merits and objective evaluation
  • Time Management: Clear scheduling and milestone-based planning

Effective team formation and management is essential for capstone project success. Teams that invest time in establishing clear communication, role definitions, and collaboration protocols will be better positioned to tackle the complex challenges of autonomous humanoid robotics.
